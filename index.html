Bhagavad Gita 2.20
Stress & Anxiety
न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः। अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे॥
na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṁ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ | ajo nityaḥ śāśvato'yaṁ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre ||
Listen to Shloka Chanting Sanskrit pronunciation & resonant temple chime

Translation & Meaning

The Soul is never born, nor does it die. It is timeless, primeval, and indestructible. It remains unharmed when outer circumstances falter.

Emotional Counter-Balancing Guidance (Vedantic Mirror)

Remember that stress and failure are temporary states of the mind. Your true essence remains untouched by outer challenges. Rest as the calm witness of this temporary distress.

Tattva 5

Dakshinamurthy Stotram: The Witness Beyond Mind & Body

"Consciousness is beyond the body and mind. Your emotions are transient mental states—you are the calm observer."

In Verse 5 of the Dakshinamurthy Stotram, Adi Shankaracharya explains that human beings mistakenly identify with their fleeting mental disturbances. When anger, euphoria, or anxiety appears, the ignorant person proclaims "I am angry" or "I am failing."

Vedanta teaches Sākṣī Bhāva (the witness state): Just as passing clouds cannot stain the azure blue of the sky, emotional turbulence belongs to the mind, never to your untouched true Self (Atman).

The Five Sheaths of Being (Pancha Koshas):
1. Annamaya Kosha: Physical Body
2. Prānamaya Kosha: Breath & Vital Energy
3. Manomaya Kosha: Emotional Mind (Where stress lives)
4. Vijñānamaya Kosha: Higher Intellect & Discernment
5. Ānandamaya Kosha: Pure Witness Bliss (Atman)
